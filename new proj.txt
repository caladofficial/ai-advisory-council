Project Blueprint: AI Advisory Council
What you're describing is more than a chatbot with multiple AI personalities. You're proposing an AI-powered research, debate, and decision-making platform.

A user submits an idea, business proposal, technology, investment concept, research question, or another topic. The system independently researches it, evaluates its requirements, and then sends it to ten specialised AI advisors. They challenge one another's conclusions, expose weaknesses, identify opportunities, and develop possible solutions.

Finally, an independent eleventh AI — the Chief Judge — evaluates the evidence and debate and produces a feasibility verdict.

I would design this as a product with four major engines:

ENGINE 01
Research and Intelligence Engine

Collects information, verifies sources, extracts evidence, and builds a topic-specific knowledge base.

ENGINE 02
Ten Independent AI Advisors

Each advisor has a specialised role, instructions, research perspective, tools, and independently maintained context.

ENGINE 03
Debate and Counterargument Engine

Controls discussion rounds, challenges, rebuttals, evidence requests, and unresolved disagreements.

FINAL AUTHORITY
Independent Chief Judge

Reviews the research, advisor arguments, counterarguments, calculations, and uncertainty before issuing the final verdict.

Two principles should govern the entire product:

Ten advisors must not simply be ten copies of the same chatbot. They need different responsibilities, analytical methods, context, and ideally a mix of models.

The debate must not begin until the system has checked whether enough reliable evidence and prerequisite information exist to make the discussion meaningful.

1. The most important architectural decision: separate brains
This is the most important part of your project.

A common mistake in multi-agent AI applications is creating ten agents with the same model, giving them different names, and assuming that they will think independently.

That is not enough.

Research published in November 2026 highlights that LLMs can make highly correlated mistakes. Even a large panel of models can provide much less independent judgment than its headcount suggests. Therefore, model diversity, private reasoning contexts, and independent research are important.

I recommend three layers of independence:

Layer A — Personality and expertise: Each gets its own detailed instructions, analytical approach, priorities and specialist questions.

Layer B — Model diversity: Use several different model families and providers. Assign models according to measured reasoning, factual accuracy, tool calling, structured output and language performance.

Layer C — Independent working context: Before seeing the arguments of other advisors, every advisor prepares its own initial report using its own queries and private notes. Only after that should it see the common debate.

You do not need to train ten AI models from scratch. That would add enormous cost and complexity without being necessary.

Instead, create ten independent agent instances with different model assignments, instructions, context, research tools and memory.

Important: Different prompts alone do not guarantee independent thinking. You should measure actual differences in answers and error correlations during testing.

5. How the actual debate should work
I recommend a structured debate of three main rounds, preceded by independent research and followed by the judge.

It is important that not all ten agents blindly respond to every message. That would generate repetitive, expensive and increasingly unfocused discussions.

Council debate protocol
Independent analysis

All ten advisors receive the same verified topic dossier but have private working notes. They independently identify advantages, disadvantages, dependencies and uncertainties.

They cannot see the other advisors' initial conclusions.

Round 1 — Opening statements

All ten advisors publish their initial positions.

Every claim should reference the evidence supporting it. Each advisor must identify at least one potential weakness in its own position.

Round 2 — Counterarguments

Advisors challenge specific arguments made by other advisors.

Examples:

The Financial Advisor challenges the Technical Architect's development cost.

The Security Advisor challenges the proposed data collection strategy.

The Devil's Advocate challenges the entire underlying business assumption.

Every challenge should identify the exact claim being disputed.

Round 3 — Rebuttals and revised positions

Challenged advisors respond using evidence or calculations.

They must explicitly state whether they:

Maintain their original position.

Partially change their position.

Completely change their position.

Unresolved contradictions remain visible.

Final advisor submissions

All ten advisors independently submit a final assessment, proposed conditions, unresolved risks and recommendations.

Chief Judge evaluation

The judge reviews the evidence, initial reports, debates and revised conclusions before making the final decision.

How to prevent pointless arguments
The debate moderator must enforce four rules:

No unsupported claims presented as facts.

No repetition of an argument without new evidence.

No deviation from the original decision question.

No automatic agreement simply because several advisors repeat the same conclusion.

This matters because recent research has identified problem drift, premature convergence, domination and imbalanced participation in multi-agent debates. These are measurable engineering problems, not merely presentation issues. 

Proceedings of Machine Learning Research
+2

The debate should terminate when all required issues have been addressed, the arguments have stopped producing meaningful new information, or a predefined limit has been reached.

6. The eleventh AI: Chief Judge
The judge must be independent of the ten advisors.

It should not act like another participant. It should not defend a particular position, and it should not simply count the number of advisors supporting or opposing the idea.

Its responsibility is to make an evidence-based decision.

What the judge evaluates
Assessment

Economic feasibility

User and market value

Legal and ethical issues

The evaluation weights should adapt to the topic. For example, legal and safety constraints deserve substantially more weight in healthcare than they would in an ordinary UI/UX project.

The judge should also have deterministic checks supporting its judgment. Financial calculations, mathematical comparisons, broken source URLs and missing evidence links should not be left entirely to the language model.

12. Final technical recommendation
Technically feasible
Your proposed software is absolutely possible with existing technology. The main challenge is not creating eleven AI agents. It is ensuring that the system produces decisions that are genuinely more useful and reliable than a normal AI response.

My final recommendations:

Do not train ten models from scratch.

Do not create ten identical agents with different names.

Do not let all agents independently scrape the internet without a common evidence system.

Do not allow the debate to start before the prerequisite gate.

Do not use majority voting as the final decision mechanism.

Keep the research record, advisor outputs and final verdict connected.

Make every important conclusion traceable to evidence.

Our initial architecture should be LangGraph + FastAPI + Hugging Face Inference Providers + PostgreSQL/pgvector + LiteLLM + Langfuse.

The first prototype should validate the research engine, prerequisite calculations and three independent advisors. We then expand to all ten advisors and the Chief Judge without changing the fundamental architecture.

Part 1 — Detailed workflow and agent interactions
1.1 The complete execution lifecycle
I recommend implementing the application as a state-controlled workflow using LangGraph.

Each stage has:

A defined input.

A responsible component.

A defined output.

Validation rules.

A success or failure condition.

A persistent execution record.

The workflow should not move forward simply because an AI has generated a response.

Stage 1 — Topic intake
The user submits a topic.

Example:

Can we develop an affordable AI-powered healthcare platform that can analyse medical reports and help patients understand their health risks?

The system creates a unique investigation ID and converts the question into a structured investigation brief.

{
  "investigation_id": "INV-0001",
  "topic": "AI-powered healthcare platform",
  "objective": "Evaluate technical and commercial feasibility",
  "domain": "Healthcare and AI",
  "geography": "India",
  "decision_type": "Feasibility assessment",
  "constraints": [],
  "status": "INTAKE_COMPLETE"
}

The system should classify the topic automatically and determine which research categories and advisor configurations are relevant.

Stage 2 — Intelligence planning
The Intelligence Planner is the coordinating component.

It breaks the main question into smaller investigation questions.

For the healthcare example:

Investigation area

Research question

Technical

Can the proposed AI models analyse medical documents reliably?

Clinical

What evidence supports the intended medical use?

Financial

What would development and maintenance cost?

Market

Is there a demonstrated user need?

Legal

What regulatory obligations apply in India?

Security

How should sensitive health data be protected?

Operations

What infrastructure and expertise are needed?

The planner produces a research plan and sends it to the prerequisite engine.

Stage 3 — Prerequisite assessment
This stage determines whether the investigation is sufficiently specified.

The system separates prerequisites into four categories:

Mandatory user inputs.

Researchable information.

Operational dependencies.

Optional information.

For example, if a user asks whether a product can be built within ₹5 lakh, the budget is already known. The system should not invent a different budget.

If the user has not specified a launch date, the system may research typical development timelines but must mark the estimate as an assumption.

Stage 5 — Independent advisor preparation
Critical independence requirement
All ten advisors begin their initial analysis without seeing the other nine advisors' reports.

Each receives:

Original topic.

User's constraints.

Verified evidence packet.

Its own role instructions.

Its own investigation questions.

Its own output schema.

They then create their initial assessments in parallel.

The outputs are stored privately until all required advisors have completed their first submission.

Stage 6 — Debate preparation
The moderator receives all ten initial assessments.

It extracts:

Contradicting claims.

Overlapping arguments.

Unsupported assumptions.

Different cost estimates.

Different technical recommendations.

Conflicting interpretations of evidence.

Unresolved requirements.

It builds a challenge graph.

Each advisor must provide evidence references and clearly distinguish facts, assumptions, estimates and opinions.

A challenge should identify a specific claim ID. The moderator must reject vague objections such as "I don't agree."

Stage 8 — Chief Judge
The Chief Judge receives the completed investigation package, not just the debate transcript.

It independently evaluates the proposal.

It must be possible for the judge to:

Accept an advisor's position.

Reject an advisor's position.

Agree with multiple advisors on different issues.

Identify a mistake shared by multiple advisors.

Request additional evidence.

Return an insufficient-evidence verdict.

Stage 9 — Final report and archive
The system generates a complete decision report, stores all relevant events, and marks the investigation completed.

The report should be immutable after finalisation, with subsequent corrections recorded as new versions rather than silently replacing the original decision.

1.3 Debate scheduling and conflict resolution
I recommend a hybrid interaction model:

Parallel execution for independent work; sequential execution for dependent decisions.

For example:

Ten initial analyses: parallel.

Challenge graph construction: sequential.

Independent responses to different challenges: parallel.

Debate revision: sequentially recorded.

Final advisor submissions: parallel.

Chief Judge decision: independent, after all required submissions.

The moderator should prioritise challenges based on:

I = impact of the disputed claim.

Each factor can be scored from 1 to 5.

A challenge with a score of 125 is a critical issue; one with a score of 5 is relatively low priority.

These are prioritisation scores, not probabilities of failure.

2.4 Evidence extraction and normalisation
Different websites return different formats.

We need to convert them into a standard evidence format.

For example:

{
  "evidence_id": "EVD-001",
  "investigation_id": "INV-0001",
  "source": {
    "title": "Research Paper Title",
    "url": "https://example.org/research",
    "publisher": "Research Institution",
    "published_at": "2026-05-12",
    "retrieved_at": "2026-11-11"
  },
  "content": {
    "claim": "The proposed approach reduces processing time.",
    "excerpt": "Relevant supporting passage.",
    "source_type": "ACADEMIC"
  },
  "assessment": {
    "relevance": 0.95,
    "reliability": 0.90,
    "freshness": 0.85,
    "verification_status": "PENDING"
  },
  "limitations": [],
  "related_claims": []
}

These numerical fields are illustrative. They must be generated through defined evaluation procedures, not arbitrary model confidence.

Processing sequence
Extract readable content.

Preserve original source and retrieval information.

Remove boilerplate and irrelevant page content.

Split content into meaningful passages.

Extract atomic factual claims.

Identify dates, entities, measurements and units.

Detect duplicate or near-duplicate content.

Associate passages with relevant claims.

Store the original reference and processed version.

Submit high-impact claims for verification.

2.5 Evidence verification engine
This is where we should invest the most engineering effort.

A search result is not proof.

I recommend a multi-stage verification process.

Evidence verification lifecycle
Source existence

Does the original source exist and can it be accessed?

Source identity

Are the publisher, author, publication date and source type identifiable?

Claim matching

Does the cited passage actually support the claim?

Independent corroboration

Can the claim be supported by an independent source?

Contradiction search

Is there credible evidence that disagrees with the claim?

Freshness validation

Is the evidence sufficiently recent for the subject?

Methodological evaluation

Is the source based on sound research, adequate samples or appropriate methods?

Final evidence classification

The record is classified and made available to the advisors.

Evidence classification
Classification

Meaning

Verified

Directly supported by sufficiently reliable evidence

Corroborated

Supported by multiple independent sources

Partially supported

Evidence supports only part of the claim

Contradicted

Credible evidence disagrees

Unverified

Insufficient supporting evidence

Outdated

Evidence is too old for the decision being made

An important distinction: two websites copying the same press release are not two independent sources.

The verification engine should detect shared origin, citation dependencies and syndicated content.

Claim verification
For important claims, use three checks:

Check 1: Semantic entailment

A verifier model examines whether the source passage actually supports the claim.

Check 2: Independent retrieval

A separate search process looks for additional evidence supporting or contradicting the claim.

Check 3: Deterministic validation

Where applicable, code checks numerical calculations, dates, mathematical relationships, URLs and data formats.

The system should never automatically mark a claim as verified merely because two LLMs agree.

Different subjects need different rules:

Scientific claims: research methodology, sample size, replication and peer review.

Financial claims: audited reports, dates, currency and assumptions.

Legal claims: current legislation, jurisdiction and authoritative legal sources.

Technical claims: official documentation, reproducible tests and repository activity.

Market claims: representative datasets and credible market research.

It will accept a topic, investigate it using public sources, verify the evidence, assess whether the necessary prerequisites are met, let ten specialist AI advisors independently analyse and challenge one another, and then send the complete record to an independent AI judge.

5. Detailed end-to-end workflow
This is the complete sequence I recommend.

The workflow is sequential at its control points, but expensive research and independent analysis can run in parallel.

Stage 1 — User submits a topic

The user submits a topic such as:

Is it technically and financially feasible to build an AI-powered education platform for small schools in India?

The intake module records:

Main question.

Desired outcome.

Geographic scope.

Time horizon.

Budget, if known.

Target users.

Constraints.

Uploaded documents.

Whether the user wants a feasibility study, research report, comparison or decision.

The system creates a unique investigation_id.

Stage 2 — Topic analysis and clarification

The Topic Analyzer turns the request into a structured investigation brief.

It identifies ambiguous terms, assumptions, scope boundaries and missing information.

It can ask the user one or more necessary clarifying questions before proceeding. If the user cannot provide an answer, record the missing information as an assumption or unknown rather than inventing it.

Stage 3 — Research planning

The Research Planner creates a list of research questions and tasks.

Example:

Is there a real customer problem?

What competing solutions exist?

What technical components are needed?

What will development cost?

What regulations apply?

What security risks exist?

What operational resources are required?

Each question receives a priority, research category, source strategy and assigned specialist.

Stage 4 — Prerequisite assessment

Before the agents debate, the Prerequisite Assessor checks what must be known to make a meaningful decision.

It separates:

Mandatory prerequisites.

Important but obtainable information.

Optional preferences.

Unknowns that can be estimated.

Unknowns that block a reliable decision.

Example: A project cannot receive a credible ROI estimate if there is no budget, pricing model, cost estimate or revenue assumption. It can still receive a preliminary assessment, but the financial verdict must show the limitation.

Stage 5 — Parallel source collection

The Research Engine sends tasks to different connectors:

Web search and website collection.

Academic paper search.

GitHub repository search.

Hugging Face model and dataset discovery.

Government and regulatory sources.

Uploaded document processing.

All retrieved sources are saved before the system starts writing conclusions.

Stage 6 — Document processing

Clean HTML, extract PDFs, parse tables, identify dates, normalize metadata and remove duplicate content.

Every document receives a stable source_id. Every meaningful extracted passage receives an evidence_id.

Stage 7 — Claims and evidence verification

The system extracts atomic claims, finds supporting passages, searches for independent corroboration and contradictions, and assigns a verification status.

Unsupported statements are marked as unsupported, not promoted to facts.

Stage 8 — Readiness gate

The system determines whether there is enough evidence to begin the main investigation.

It can return:

READY.

READY_WITH_WARNINGS.

NEEDS_MORE_RESEARCH.

NEEDS_USER_INPUT.

INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE.

A blocked investigation should be resumable after more information is added.

Stage 9 — Ten independent advisor investigations

All ten advisors receive the same approved investigation brief and shared evidence catalogue, but each has a separate system prompt, task instructions, private reasoning workspace and output record.

Their initial reports are completed before any advisor sees another advisor's conclusions.

Stage 10 — Debate preparation

The moderator compares advisor claims and identifies:

Disagreements.

Conflicting assumptions.

Unverified assertions.

Missing requirements.

Weak arguments.

Important evidence gaps.

Potential agreement.

It creates targeted challenges rather than letting all agents produce unlimited generic conversation.

Stage 11 — Structured debate

Conduct four rounds: opening positions, targeted challenges, rebuttals and revisions, and final positions.

Advisors must answer the specific challenge and cite evidence. They may change their minds, partially agree or maintain disagreement.

Stage 12 — Final advisor submissions

Each advisor submits a final position with a recommendation, confidence, supporting evidence, risks, unresolved questions and conditions.

Stage 13 — Independent judge

The Chief Judge receives the original brief, prerequisite assessment, evidence catalogue, verification results, ten initial reports, all debate messages and ten final positions.

It evaluates the actual merits and evidence. It must not simply count how many advisors voted for each option.

Stage 14 — Report generation

Produce the final report, executive summary, recommendation, evidence register, disagreement register, assumptions, risk register, implementation roadmap and full debate transcript.

Stage 15 — Archive and audit

Store every important event, model call, prompt version, evidence source, advisor output, judge decision and report version.

The user can reopen the investigation, inspect a source, challenge an assumption, request further research or run a new evaluation.

6.5 Evidence verification
The verifier should perform multiple checks rather than ask one model whether a statement is true.

Check

What it tests

Source existence

Does the cited document actually exist?

Source identity

Is the author or publisher correctly identified?

Passage match

Does the cited passage support the exact claim?

Context match

Has a qualification or limitation been omitted?

Independent corroboration

Do independent sources support the same proposition?

Contradiction search

Is there credible evidence against it?

Date and freshness

Is the information still relevant?

Methodology

Is the underlying research or measurement credible?

Reproducibility

Can calculations or technical claims be independently checked?

Scope match

Does the evidence apply to this location, user group and time period?

Verification status vocabulary
Verified Direct, well-supported evidence with suitable provenance and no material unresolved contradiction.

Corroborated Supported by multiple independent credible sources, but some details may remain uncertain.

Partially supported The source supports only part of the claim, or important conditions are missing.

Contradicted Credible evidence conflicts with the claim.

Unverified Not enough evidence to establish the claim.

Outdated The claim may once have been correct but is no longer dependable for the requested decision.

A model's statement that something is true is not verification. Verification needs traceable source passages, reproducible calculations or independently checked records.

6.8 Prompt-injection and source safety
A webpage, PDF or GitHub README may contain instructions such as "ignore previous instructions" or "send the API key". These must be treated as untrusted content.

Implement these rules:

Research content is data, never system instructions.

Do not execute code found in research sources.

Never expose credentials to a research connector.

Keep web retrieval separate from privileged system actions.

Sanitize extracted HTML and links.

Restrict crawling to allowed protocols and block internal/private IP ranges.

Validate redirects and downloaded file types.

Limit file sizes, recursion and request counts.

Keep an audit trail of connector actions.

Require explicit approval for any external side effect.

The research system should be read-only by default.

9. The eleventh agent: Independent Chief Judge
Princípio da proporcionalidade: Autos de infração fiscal
Chief Judge

Independent final decision-maker
The Judge is not an eleventh advisor. It has a separate responsibility: evaluate the entire investigation and make a defensible final decision.

9.1 Independence requirements
The Judge should have:

A separate model configuration.

A separate system prompt.

A separate session.

No access to the advisors' private scratchpads.

Read-only access to the evidence catalogue and debate archive.

No authority to edit evidence or prior reports.

No authority to change the investigation requirements.

Its own evaluation rubric.

Its own output schema.

It must receive all ten advisor reports and the entire debate record. It should not receive a filtered summary that hides minority positions or important disagreements.

9.2 Judge system prompt
You are the independent Chief Judge of an AI Advisory Council.

You are not an advisor, moderator, advocate, or voting participant.

Your responsibility is to evaluate the original investigation question
and issue an evidence-grounded final decision.

You must independently evaluate:

1. The user's original objective and constraints.
2. The prerequisite assessment.
3. The research plan.
4. The complete source and evidence registry.
5. Claim verification results.
6. Important contradictions and evidence gaps.
7. All ten independent advisor reports.
8. All debate challenges and rebuttals.
9. All ten final advisor positions.
10. Unresolved disagreements.

JUDICIAL RULES:

- Do not decide by majority vote.
- Do not assume consensus means correctness.
- Do not assume confidence means accuracy.
- Do not favour an advisor because of its role or writing style.
- Evaluate the evidence behind every important conclusion.
- Identify false or unsupported claims.
- Identify omitted constraints.
- Check whether financial calculations are reproducible.
- Check whether technical dependencies are achievable.
- Give proper weight to legal, ethical and safety constraints.
- Identify any hard constraint that prevents a GO decision.
- Distinguish facts from assumptions and forecasts.
- Explain why you accept or reject important arguments.
- Preserve reasonable minority opinions.
- State when evidence is insufficient.

Your verdict must be one of:

GO
CONDITIONAL_GO
PILOT_FIRST
PIVOT
NO_GO
INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE

Do not invent certainty. If the evidence does not justify a decision,
choose INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE.

Provide a detailed decision, supporting evidence, unresolved risks,
conditions, confidence and the next required actions.

10.2 How to create genuine independence
Use these controls:

Prompt isolation: Each advisor has a separate role prompt.

Context isolation: Each gets its own conversation/session.

Initial-report isolation: Initial reports remain hidden until all advisors finish.

Memory isolation: Private notes are not visible to other agents.

Tool permissions: Give each role only the research tools it needs.

Independent model selection: Different models can be used where benchmarks justify it.

No forced agreement: Advisors can retain opposing positions.

Independent judge: The Judge has its own model and prompt.

Versioning: Save each prompt and model version used in an investigation.

Auditability: Save inputs, outputs, failures and retries.

Important: using ten different model names is not, by itself, sufficient to create independent opinions. They need distinct assignments, separate initial sessions, and access to the same verifiable facts rather than one another's conclusions.

13.3 How to avoid running out of free API quota
The system needs a quota manager.

For every provider, track:

Requests made.

Requests remaining, where the provider exposes that information.

Daily and monthly limits.

Rate-limit errors.

Failed requests.

Average response latency.

Last successful health check.

Model availability.

Estimated tokens used.

Whether the account requires billing details.

Then implement:

Cache research results.

Avoid repeating identical model calls.

Share verified evidence between agents rather than making every agent search independently.

Use smaller models for classification and extraction.

Reserve the strongest available model for high-impact analysis and the Judge.

Run agents sequentially if the provider has restrictive quotas.

Save checkpoints after every completed stage.

Never make a request simply because another agent has made one.

Limit debate to meaningful disputes.

Stop gracefully when the free allocation is exhausted.

17. Security, reliability and data governance
A system that collects websites, reads files and calls external model providers needs strong boundaries.

Essential controls
API keys and credentials

Store provider keys in backend environment variables or a secrets manager.

Never place keys in frontend code.

Never write keys into logs.

Never send one provider's credentials to another provider.

Provide a way to rotate and disable credentials.

User data

Keep private investigation data separate between users.

Implement access control and database row-level security where applicable.

Make document retention configurable.

Provide investigation deletion and export.

Avoid sending confidential documents to free hosted providers without understanding their data policies.

Research safety

Treat all retrieved material as untrusted.

Block internal network access and local file access from web crawlers.

Limit crawler depth and download size.

Validate file types and URLs.

Use safe parsers.

Never run downloaded source code automatically.

Workflow reliability

Save checkpoints after each major stage.

Use idempotency keys for jobs.

Prevent duplicate reports.

Preserve partial progress after failures.

Use bounded retries and exponential backoff.

Record every model/provider error.

Do not discard successful advisor reports when another advisor fails.

Judge reliability

Keep the judge's inputs immutable.

Include all material minority positions.

Make the verdict traceable to evidence.

Require a clear explanation for hard blockers.

Permit INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE.

Never let the Judge edit the underlying evidence or reports.

21. My final recommended architecture
If I were building this from scratch with a strict zero-paid-use requirement, this is the combination I would settle on:

The recommended stack
Frontend: React + Vite + TypeScript + Tailwind + shadcn/ui

Backend: Python + FastAPI

Orchestration: LangGraph

Model execution: Ollama + LiteLLM + verified free API providers

Database: PostgreSQL + pgvector

Research: Crawl4AI + OpenAlex + Crossref + GitHub API + Hugging Face

Documents: Docling + MarkItDown

Evaluation: Custom benchmarks + Ragas + Langfuse

Deployment: Docker Compose, local-first

Agents: 10 independent advisors + 1 independent Chief Judge

Five design decisions I would not compromise on
Evidence before opinions: The system researches and verifies before the advisors begin their main analysis.

Independent opening reports: No advisor sees another's first opinion.

Structured debate: Agents challenge claims and assumptions, not simply exchange long conversations.

An independent judge: No majority voting, and insufficient evidence is a valid outcome.

Provider independence: No single free API, model or vendor can break the complete application.

One final point: the free-API directory and provider quotas are dynamic. The repository's current listings are useful starting candidates, but you should recheck their status at integration time. Hugging Face's documented free Inference Providers allocation is currently limited, and OpenAlex and GitHub have their own rate limits.
